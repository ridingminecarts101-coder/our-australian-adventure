# Oceania access exceptions and editorial review

Reviewed 2026-09-11. The 37-row minimum is met, but the ratios for countries with only two to five underlying rows can look stronger than the practical depth of their visitor offer. These are explicit human-review notes, not claims that access is routine.

## Restricted and fragile access

### Tokelau (TK)

Retain `Nukunonu village guest stay` only with its advance-approval language. The [Government of Tokelau tourist information](https://www.tokelau.org.nz/Tokelau%2BGovernment/Visiting%2BTokelau/Tourist%2BInformation.html) says there is no established tourist industry, access is by sea from Apia, and accommodation is private village guest housing or local homes. The [government permit page](https://www.tokelau.org.nz/Tokelau%2BGovernment/Government%2BDepartments/BDM%2BCertificates.html) requires foreign visitors to obtain an entry permit and contact the service a month before travel; approval comes from the relevant village. Shipping schedules can change and must not be presented as guaranteed. Editorial status: **retain with prominent access caveat; recheck before release**.

### Pitcairn Islands (PN)

`St Paul's Pool` was removed after the qualitative pass because Pitcairn Islands Tourism calls it one of the island's most striking natural attractions; remoteness alone does not make that flagship site a hidden gem. The interim `Aute Valley tennis court` row was then removed in bounded final QA because an ordinary sports court was not sufficiently destination-specific or memory-creating. The final `Down Rope guided hike` is explicitly documented by [Pitcairn Islands Tourism](https://www.visitpitcairn.pn/local-services-activities): a local-guide-only descent beneath a roughly 700-foot cliff to a sandy beach and ancient Polynesian petroglyphs. The [official visa and legal page](https://www.visitpitcairn.pn/visa-legal) says there is no airport, medical evacuation cover is mandatory, and the island is steep and rugged. Editorial status: **retain only with the official guide-only and danger language; never present Down Rope as an independent hike**.

### Tuvalu (TV)

`Nanumea WWII airfield remains` was removed in bounded final QA. The Government of Tuvalu page establishes that remains exist, but it offers no bookable, guided or otherwise defined visitor route. [Australia's Department of Defence](https://www.defence.gov.au/news-events/news/2026-07-20/reassurance-one-step-time) also reported in July 2026 that dangerous wartime bombs remain around Nanumea and that many exact locations are unknown. “Confirm locally” is not enough to turn that hazard evidence into a supported experience. The replacement `Funafuti runway community games` is directly invited on the [Government of Tuvalu accommodation page](https://tourism.gov.tv/accommodation/): visitors may join locals for late-afternoon games or walking once the runway is cleared. Editorial status: **retain only with the runway-cleared and obey-airport-instructions conditions**.

### Nauru (NR)

The originally drafted `Ganakoro cave tour` was removed because the Nauru Tourism hostname no longer resolved during the final access audit. A generic self-drive replacement was then rejected in the qualitative pass because transport is not itself a hidden attraction. The final `Naoero Museum` row uses the live [Government of Naoero museum page](https://www.nauru.gov.nr/government/departments/nauru-museum.aspx). Its limited weekday hours make advance confirmation important. Editorial status: **retain as a small, overlooked cultural stop; recheck opening before release**.

### Wallis & Futuna (WF)

The former territorial portal was under live maintenance during the final audit, so it is not used as provenance. `Talietumu fortified site` was also removed because the current tourism office treats the fort as a must-do stop. The replacement `Uvea American trail` comes from the [Wallis and Futuna Tourism Office suggested route](https://www.wallis-futuna.travel/en/what-to-see-do/suggested-tours), which describes dispersed wartime traces and recommends the Uvea Museum Association for interpretation. Editorial status: **retain as an interpretation-dependent themed trail; arrange guidance and confirm site access**.

### Federated States of Micronesia (FM)

FSM Tourism explicitly asks visitors to obtain the nearby landowner's permission before using the Sipyen Waterfall path. The row preserves that requirement and cautions about slippery rocks. Editorial status: **retain only with the permission wording**.

## Small-scale or operator-dependent rows

- PG `Namatanai surf coast`: specialist retreat/guide dependent; current swell and transport must be checked.
- PG `POM City Markets`: venue is periodic and can change; do not hard-code a calendar or address.
- SB `Vilu War Museum`: check opening and road transport locally; the official tourism page establishes the site but not a current timetable.
- SB `Mataniko Cascade trek`: local guide required editorially because the source identifies an inland trek but does not provide a maintained independent route.
- VU `Losinwei Cascades walk`: locally guided route with rock pools and a steep rock face; conditions and guide confirmation are essential.
- VU `Mount Taputoara guided trek`: community access and local guide should remain explicit.
- TO `Fangatave Beach trail`: official tourism support is brief; keep flagged for route and land-access confirmation.
- CK `Takitumu Conservation Area guided tour`: published group size is two to five and only selected weekdays; do not generalise it into daily open access.
- PW `Belau Eco Glass Center`: confirm visitor or demonstration access before release because it is inside the Koror waste-management facility.
- GU `Pågat Cave trail`: the official listing verifies the cave pool and jungle hike but gives little safety detail; the guide/light/heavy-rain wording is intentionally conservative.

## Borderline hidden-gem classification checks

The following meet the present rationale standard but deserve final editorial eyes because each destination's overall visitor circuit is already small: Vilu War Museum (SB), Fort Teremba (NC), 'Ene'io Botanical Garden (TO), Alele Museum (MH), and Historic Inalåhan (GU). Their rationales rely on small scale, specialist interpretation, a distinct detour, or distance from the destination's headline experience—not simply remoteness.

## Qualitative hidden-gem corrections

The second-pass test rejected any row whose only defensible rationale was that its whole country or territory receives few visitors. Five rows were initially replaced without changing country counts:

- VU: `Millennium Cave canyon tour` was rejected because Vanuatu Tourism calls it Santo's most famous natural attraction; `Losinwei Cascades walk` is the smaller Malekula route.
- WS: `Falealupo Canopy Walkway` was rejected as an established named attraction; Samoa Tourism explicitly calls replacement `Sauniatu Waterfall` a hidden gem.
- PN: `St Paul's Pool` was rejected because the official source calls it one of Pitcairn's most striking natural attractions.
- NR: generic `Naoero island self-drive` was rejected as transport rather than a discrete experience; `Naoero Museum` supplies a small, limited-hours cultural stop outside the usual outdoor circuit.
- WF: `Talietumu fortified site` was rejected because the tourism office presents it as a must-do; `Uvea American trail` is a dispersed, guide-enhanced heritage theme.

Bounded final QA then made two further replacements: ordinary `Aute Valley tennis court` became the destination-specific, guide-only `Down Rope guided hike`, while unsupported `Nanumea WWII airfield remains` became the officially documented `Funafuti runway community games`. The remaining small-island rows have an experience-specific basis beyond national obscurity: an interior route rather than Niue's promoted coastal chasms (`Vinivini Track`); a pre-approved, non-commercial village stay rather than a conventional attraction (Tokelau); a schedule-dependent shared local ritual on working transport infrastructure (Funafuti runway games); or a small cultural/community facility that contrasts with the destination's dominant activity set (Alele Museum and the Kiribati homestay programme).

No unsupported safety, opening-time, price, dog-access or precise seasonal claim was intentionally added. `verified_at` records confirmation of the core place/experience, not guaranteed future operation. Mutable details remain phrased as checks.

## Final source-access audit

Every URL retained in `oceania-provenance.jsonl` was opened and its supporting text read on 2026-09-11. The final Pitcairn activity page and Government of Tuvalu accommodation page were reopened during bounded QA and directly support Down Rope and cleared-runway community use. The three Tourism Fiji pages rejected the text research fetcher with HTTP 403, so they were separately opened and read in the rendered in-app browser; all three displayed the claims recorded in provenance. The superseded New Caledonia PDF failed certificate validation and the superseded Nauru Tourism hostname did not resolve, so neither remains in the provenance file. They were replaced respectively by the live New Caledonia Tourism Deva page and the live Government of Naoero museum page. The territorial Wallis and Futuna portal was under maintenance, so the retained American-trail row uses the live Wallis and Futuna Tourism Office suggested-tour page. No retained record depends only on a search snippet or an inaccessible page.
